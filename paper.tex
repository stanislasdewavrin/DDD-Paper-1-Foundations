\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We define a deterministic, parallel, local update rule on a
discrete graph. Each node carries a non-negative reserve
$R_i \ge 0$, with rest value $R_0$ at equilibrium. Each
oriented link carries a non-negative directional flux. The rule
alternates a node update and a link update, with the outgoing
fluxes from a node rate-limited so that the reserve cannot
become negative. The
internal exchange of reserve between nodes is exactly
conservative; external sinks are explicit bookkeeping terms.
The rule is non-linear only by way of the non-negativity of
the reserve, which acts as a finite resource. In its linearised regime, far from empty, the rule's
steady state coincides with the discrete Poisson equation, and
we measure on the lattice that the static profile around a
point source follows a $1/r$ law with the continuum coefficient
at the percent level. The contribution of this paper is the
rule, not the $1/r$ law: any discrete diffusion in three
dimensions yields $1/r$ in the linearised steady state, but the
finite-resource, two-step structure of the present rule is what
differentiates it from prior lattice models, and is what carries
the saturating consequences explored in the companion papers.
We are explicit about what we do not derive in this paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{What this paper claims}
\label{sec:claims}

We make three claims.

\begin{enumerate}
\item We define a deterministic, parallel, two-step local rule
on a discrete graph. The reserve is bounded below by zero
($R_i \ge 0$, the physical floor: one cannot drain what is not
there); $R_0$ is the rest value, but the reserve is free to
exceed it in excited regimes, treated in companion papers. The
rule is not the discrete Poisson equation by construction: it
is a non-linear update that reduces to the linearised Poisson
equation only when the reserve stays well above zero
everywhere.
\item Run as a stationary linearised simulation with a point
source on a cubic lattice, the rule produces a radial profile
compatible with $1/r$ in the bulk, with a coefficient that
matches the discrete continuum prediction at the percent level
across $L \in \{64, 128, 160\}$.
\item The internal flux-exchange rule preserves total reserve
exactly. The non-negativity of the reserve is enforced by
rate-limiting the outgoing fluxes and external sinks of a node
in proportion, so that emission stops when the reserve reaches
zero; no mass is created or destroyed by the internal rule.
This is the structural feature that distinguishes the rule from
a plain discrete heat equation. (The full rule contains a
dormant gauge sector with its own invariants, not used in this
paper.)
\end{enumerate}

We do not claim the following. We do not derive Newton's
gravitational constant in physical units; we calibrate it. We
do not derive general relativity. We do not derive any property
of the Standard Model. We do not show that the rule is unique.
We do not assert that the $1/r$ result is itself novel: it is
not. What is novel is the rule, and the structural features
(reserve non-negativity, rest level $R_0$, two-step
alternation, rate-limited fluxes) that make the rule non-trivial in the
regimes explored in the companion papers.

\section{The picture, in plain words}
\label{sec:picture}

Imagine a fine three-dimensional grid. At each grid point sits
a reservoir holding a non-negative amount of stuff. There is a
rest level $R_0$ that the reservoirs all sit at when the system
is undisturbed; reservoirs can be excited above $R_0$ or
drained below it, but they cannot go below zero. Between
neighbours run pipes that carry a non-negative flow of stuff
from the fuller reservoir to the emptier one, at a rate
proportional to the level difference. A reservoir cannot
deliver more stuff than it actually has: a node at zero stops
emitting. There is no separate ``maximum flow rate'' parameter;
the cap follows from what the reservoir contains.

If we start the system at the rest level $R_0$ everywhere,
drain one point, and let the system settle, the bulk reservoir
levels relax into a stationary pattern. Far from the source,
where every reservoir remains well above zero, the pattern
reduces to the textbook discrete Poisson solution and the
deficit $R_0 - R_i$ falls as $1/r$ with distance.

The interesting cases are where the floor matters. Near a
strong drainage point, the reservoir level can be driven to
zero, and emission from that node stops. In this regime the
rule is no longer a discrete heat equation: it is a non-linear
update with a finite-resource bound. The companion papers
explore those regimes (compact-object saturation in Paper~II,
cosmogenetic regime in Paper~III, gauge sector in
Papers~V--VI). The present paper is restricted to the
linearised regime, where the rule reproduces $1/r$.

The rest of this paper makes the rule precise.

\section{The substrate}
\label{sec:substrate}

\subsection{Graph and variables}

The substrate is a graph $G = (V, E)$ in which every node has
at least six neighbours and the connectivity is statistically
isotropic at large scale. We do not specify the exact topology;
in our simulations we use a regular cubic lattice for clarity,
and we have verified that BCC gives the same large-scale
spectral dimension.

For the purposes of this paper, each node $i \in V$ carries a
non-negative reserve $R_i \ge 0$, with rest value $R_0$ at
equilibrium. Each oriented link $(i, j) \in E$ carries a pair
of non-negative directional reserve fluxes $F_{i \to j} \ge 0$
and $F_{j \to i} \ge 0$. These are the only variables that
enter the linearised gravitational simulation reported here.
The non-negativity $R_i \ge 0$ is the physical floor (one
cannot drain what is not there) and is the only structural
source of non-linearity in the rule. In the static
gravitational regime treated below, $R$ stays at or below
$R_0$ everywhere because the only source acts as a sink; in
excited regimes (companion papers) $R$ can exceed $R_0$.

\subsection*{Variables of the wider rule}

The full rule, as used in the companion papers, also assigns to
each node two angular quadratures $(T_i, I_i)$ subject to a
local coherence bound $T_i^2 + I_i^2 \le R_i$, and to each
oriented link a phase $\psi_{ij} \in [-\pi, \pi)$ with
$\psi_{ji} = -\psi_{ij}$. These additional variables drive the
gauge sector (Papers~V--VI), couple to the reserve through the
coherence bound, and do not enter the static linearised
gravitational regime treated below. We mention them here for
completeness; we do not invoke them in the simulation or in the
results of this paper.

\section{The two-step tick}
\label{sec:tick}

\subsection*{Motivation}

The class of rules considered here is motivated by an old line
of inquiry on whether physics admits a microscopic
deterministic, local, iterative description --- a
``computational substrate'' in the sense of Zuse, Fredkin,
't~Hooft, Wolfram, and Lloyd
\cite{zuse1969,fredkin1990,thooft2016,wolfram2002,lloyd2006}.
We do not commit to the strong form of this hypothesis. We
adopt it as a working stance: it tells us what shape a
candidate substrate rule should have --- deterministic, local,
parallel, conservative, with bounded transport --- and the
present paper studies one such rule. The numerical claim of
the paper does not depend on the working stance.

\subsection*{The rule}

The rule decomposes into two alternating half-steps, which we
call \emph{Node Update} and \emph{Link Update}. The choice of
two-step alternation is a standard one for local lattice
theories: it preserves locality (each update reads only from
nearest neighbours) and avoids self-reference within a tick. We
do not derive the alternation from a deeper principle; we adopt
it as a construction.

\subsection{Link Update}

During the first half-tick, the node reserves are frozen. For
each oriented link $(i, j)$, in parallel, we compute a desired
flux,
\begin{equation}
F_{i \to j}^{\rm des} \;=\; \alpha\, (R_i - R_j)_+,
\label{eq:fluxdes}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha$ the propagation coefficient. The two directions
are independent. Each node then computes a rate-limiting factor
that ensures the reserve stays non-negative on its outgoing
side. Define the available local budget and the desired total
demand,
\begin{equation}
A_i \;=\; R_i \,+\, \tau\, S_i^{\rm in},
\qquad
S_i^{\rm in} = \sum_j F_{j \to i}^{\rm des},
\qquad
D_i \;=\; \tau\, O_i^{\rm des} + \tau\, \kappa\, E_i,
\qquad
O_i^{\rm des} = \sum_j F_{i \to j}^{\rm des},
\label{eq:budgets}
\end{equation}
where the demand $D_i$ counts \emph{both} the desired total
outflow and the local sink, since both draw from the same
reserve. The rate-limiter is
\begin{equation}
\beta_i \;=\;
\begin{cases}
1, & D_i = 0,\\[2pt]
\min\!\left(1,\; A_i / D_i \right), & D_i > 0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
and is applied identically to the outflows and to the sink:
\begin{equation}
F_{i \to j} \;=\; \beta_i\, F_{i \to j}^{\rm des},
\qquad
E_i^{\rm real} \;=\; \beta_i\, E_i.
\label{eq:flux}
\end{equation}
When demand exceeds the available budget, $\beta_i < 1$ scales
outflows and sink down in the same proportion. When the reserve
is comfortably above zero, $\beta_i = 1$ and the rule is linear.

\subsection{Node Update}

During the second half-tick, the link fluxes are frozen. For
each node $i$, in parallel,
\begin{equation}
R_i \;\leftarrow\; R_i + \tau\, S_i^{\rm in}
\,-\, \beta_i\, \tau\, O_i^{\rm des}
\,-\, \beta_i\, \tau\, \kappa\, E_i.
\label{eq:nodeR}
\end{equation}
By construction of $\beta_i$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:beta}, this
update keeps $R_i$ non-negative at every tick, without any
clipping by hand. The internal exchange of reserve between
nodes is exactly conservative; the external sources $E_i$
enter as explicit bookkeeping terms (see
Sec.~\ref{sec:conservation}).

\subsection{Why this is not the discrete heat equation}

In the regime where every node has a comfortable available
budget, $\beta_i = 1$ everywhere, and the rule reduces to a
discrete heat equation. Outside that regime, it does not: a
node whose available local budget $A_i = R_i + \tau S_i^{\rm
in}$ falls short of its desired demand $D_i$ has $\beta_i < 1$,
and its outflows and sink are scaled down in proportion. A
node at zero reserve with no incoming compensation stops
emitting altogether. The desired outflow is not realised; the
reserve does not become negative; no mass is created or lost. This non-linearity is not present
in the standard discrete heat equation. It is the structural
feature that the companion papers exploit (Paper~II for
compact-object saturation, Paper~III for cosmogenetic regimes).

In terms of the deficit $\delta_i = R_0 - R_i$, the desired flux
reads $F_{i \to j}^{\rm des} = \alpha\, (\delta_j - \delta_i)_+$:
the reserve flows from the fuller site toward the emptier one.

In the static gravitational regime treated below, where
$R \le R_0$ everywhere, the bound on the flux satisfies
$0 \le F_{i \to j} \le \alpha R_0$. This is a downstream
consequence of the initial conditions and source type, not a
separate cap imposed on the rule.

\section{Conservation}
\label{sec:conservation}

\textbf{Total reserve, exactly conserved by internal exchange.}
The actual realised fluxes $F_{i \to j} = \beta_i\, F_{i \to
j}^{\rm des}$ enter the Node Update of $j$ as
$+ \beta_i F_{i \to j}^{\rm des}$ (incoming) and the Node
Update of $i$ as $-\beta_i F_{i \to j}^{\rm des}$ (outgoing).
The two contributions cancel when summed over the whole graph,
so $\sum_i R_i$ is exactly conserved by the inter-node flux
dynamics. The rate-limiter $\beta_i$ does not violate
conservation: it scales outflows and the local sink in the same
proportion, so any reduction of an outgoing flux at $i$ is
matched by a reduction of the absorbed energy
$E_i^{\rm real} = \beta_i E_i$, never by a creation or
destruction of reserve.

\textbf{External sinks as bookkeeping.} The total reserve in
the network changes only by $\sum_i E_i^{\rm real}$, which
counts what is taken out by external sources. The closed
system consisting of the network plus the external sink
reservoir is conservative; the network alone is open, and the
sink terms are explicit accounting of that openness, not a
violation of conservation.

\textbf{Dormant gauge sector.} The full rule contains a
link-phase $\psi_{ij}$ whose closed-loop circulation modulo
$2\pi$ is preserved by the topology. This invariant is not
active in the gravitational regime studied here.

\section{Simulation: from rule to $1/r$}
\label{sec:sim}

\subsection{Derivation of the Poisson limit from the rule}

In the linear regime $\beta_i = 1$ everywhere, and the desired
fluxes coincide with the realised fluxes. The two directional
fluxes on a link satisfy
\[
F_{i \to j} - F_{j \to i}
\;=\; \alpha\,(R_i - R_j)_+ - \alpha\,(R_j - R_i)_+
\;=\; \alpha\,(R_i - R_j),
\]
since exactly one of the two terms is non-zero. The net
inflow into node $i$ is therefore
\[
S_i = \sum_{j \sim i} (F_{j \to i} - F_{i \to j})
    = \alpha \sum_{j \sim i} (R_j - R_i)
    = \alpha\, \Delta R_i,
\]
where $\Delta f(i) = \sum_{j \sim i} f(j) - n_i\, f(i)$ is the
discrete graph Laplacian (with $n_i = 6$ on cubic). Writing
the Node Update~\eqref{eq:nodeR} in stationary regime and
dividing by $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
\alpha\, \Delta R_i \;=\; \kappa\, E_i.
\label{eq:stationary}
\end{equation}
Switching to the deficit $\delta_i = R_0 - R_i$, since $R_0$ is
constant we have $\Delta \delta_i = -\Delta R_i$, and
\begin{equation}
-\alpha\, \Delta\, \delta_i \;=\; \kappa\, E_i.
\label{eq:poisson_general}
\end{equation}
For a single point source $E_i = E_0\, \delta_{\rm Kr}(i, i_0)$,
this is the discrete Poisson equation
\begin{equation}
-\Delta\, \delta_i \;=\; M\, \delta_{\rm Kr}(i, i_0),
\qquad
M \;\equiv\; \frac{\kappa\, E_0}{\alpha},
\label{eq:poisson}
\end{equation}
with source strength $M$ set by the imposed sink magnitude
$E_0$ and the microscopic ratio $\kappa/\alpha$. The continuum solution is
$\delta(r) = M/(4\pi r)$ since
$\Delta(1/r) = -4\pi\, \delta^{(3)}(r)$ in 3D. This is the
$1/r$ profile we now measure on the lattice.

Laplacian on cubic and BCC at $L \in \{32, 64, 128\}$ via the
heat-kernel return probability
$P(t) = \langle e^{-\lambda t}\rangle$, with
$d_s(t) = -2\,{\rm d}\log P / {\rm d}\log t$. The plateau value
of $d_s$ converges to $3$ as $L$ grows. The residual $d_s - 3$

\section{Calibration vs.\ prediction}
\label{sec:cal-vs-pred}

\textbf{Calibration.} The mapping between the dimensionless
deficit field $\delta_i$ and the physical Newtonian potential
$\Phi$, and the mapping between the local energy budget $E_i$
and physical mass, are external calibrations: in this paper,
$\delta$ and $E$ remain dimensionless lattice quantities. The
lattice unit of length and the parameter $\kappa$ are fixed by
demanding that the coefficient $A_{\rm fit}$, when matched to
Newton's law, reproduces the measured $G$ for a chosen test
mass. This is one calibration condition for two parameters, and
it fixes a one-parameter family of physical realisations.

\textbf{Prediction.} Within that family, the rule predicts that
the static potential of an isolated point mass is $1/r$ at
distances on which the linearised regime is valid, and that
finite-resource departures from $1/r$ appear when the bounds
become active.

We separate these two cleanly throughout the companion papers.
``DDD agrees with X'' means prediction; ``DDD is calibrated
against Y'' means calibration.

\section{Relation to existing work}
\label{sec:related}

The single-point claim of this paper, that a local rule
produces a $1/r$ static potential, is by itself trivial: any
diffusive process in three dimensions has the same property,
and the discrete graph Laplacian's Green function is a textbook
result. The contribution of the paper is the \emph{rule}, not
the result. We position it briefly against neighbouring
literatures.

\textbf{Lattice gauge theory} \cite{wilson1974}. The two-step
alternation of node and link updates mirrors the standard
matter-gauge structure of Wilson lattice gauge theories, and
the link-phase $\psi_{ij}$ in the wider rule plays the role of
a $\mathrm{U}(1)$ gauge variable. The distinction here is that
the link sector carries a directional reserve flux
$F_{i \to j}$, automatically bounded by the non-negativity of
the reserve. The presence of a finite-resource floor on a
propagating quantity is not standard in lattice gauge theory
and is what the companion papers exploit.

\textbf{Cellular automata for physics}
\cite{wolfram2002,thooft2016}. Local deterministic rules on
discrete substrates have a long history. Most CA programmes
target unification of multiple sectors of physics from a single
substrate. The present paper is more modest: we define one rule
and one regime, and we do not claim more.

\textbf{Causal sets and discrete-spacetime gravity}
\cite{bombelli1987,ambjorn2005}. These programmes treat the
substrate itself as dynamical (the graph or simplicial complex
evolves). Here the substrate is fixed; the dynamics live on
its node and link variables.

\textbf{Discrete diffusion and graph Laplacian}
\cite{chung1997}. The static linear regime of our rule is the
discrete Poisson equation, and the $1/r$ result is its direct
consequence. We do not claim novelty here. The contribution is
the embedding of this linear regime inside a non-linear,
bounded, two-step rule that admits non-trivial behaviour
outside the linear regime. The numerical result in
Sec.~\ref{sec:sim} should accordingly be read as a validation
of the implementation and of the linear regime under the
specified initial and boundary conditions, not as a new
theorem about graph Laplacians.

\textbf{Entropic and thermodynamic gravity}
\cite{verlinde2011,padmanabhan2010}. These derive Newton's law
from thermodynamic identities on horizons. The present rule is
a microscopic mechanical model, not a thermodynamic
derivation. The two are not in tension; they target different
levels of description.

\textbf{Lattice fluid models} \cite{benzi1992}. Lattice
Boltzmann methods are conservative, local, parallel updates of
density and momentum fields with caps and floors built in for
stability. The structural similarity to the present rule is
real, and we use lattice-Boltzmann-style implementation
techniques in the simulation. The physical interpretation is
different: lattice Boltzmann targets fluid mechanics, the
present rule targets a substrate from which gravitational and
gauge-like behaviour are intended to emerge.

\section{What the model is not, and what it does not do}
\label{sec:not}

The rule is Newtonian in its present static linearised form.
We do not derive general relativity in this paper, and the
companion paper on the weak-field limit (Paper~II) is a
weak-field limit, not a derivation of the full Einstein
equations.

We do not derive Newton's gravitational constant in physical
units; we calibrate it. A research programme to derive it is
sketched in Paper~II.

We do not derive the fine-structure constant, particle masses,
or any property of the Standard Model.

We do not specify the exact graph topology beyond ``minimum 6
neighbours, statistically isotropic''. We have tested cubic and
BCC, and they agree at the percent level.

We do not give a microscopic origin for the reserve rest value
$R_0$, which sets the structural scale of the rule. The floor
at zero is fixed by the physical statement that one cannot
drain what is not there.

We have not shown that the rule is unique. There may exist
other local, conservative, bounded rules that produce a $1/r$
profile in their linearised regime.

\section{Open questions}
\label{sec:open}

\paragraph{Why six neighbours?} Six neighbours is the minimal
coordination of the cubic stencil used in our simulation; it is
not a fundamental requirement of the rule. More general graphs
require separate checks of spectral dimension and isotropy. We
have no microscopic argument for why the substrate would prefer
any specific coordination.

\paragraph{Why this order, Node then Link?} The two orderings
are not equivalent: $U_{\rm tick}$ is not invariant under
exchange. This is a microscopic breaking of time-reversal. We
have no microscopic argument for the choice.

\paragraph{What sets $R_0$?} $R_0$ is the rest reserve level
at equilibrium, the only structural scale of the rule. The
floor at zero is fixed physically. We do not derive $R_0$.

\paragraph{Is the rule unique?} See Sec.~\ref{sec:not}.

\paragraph{What sits below the substrate?} The graph is the
lowest level of description here. Whether it is reducible to
anything more fundamental is beyond the present paper.

\section{Outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

The companion papers explore further consequences of the same
local rule. We mark each with our own assessment of how
well-supported its claims are.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Paper~II --- weak-field gravitational behaviour,
including saturation effects when the reserve approaches zero
near compact sources. \emph{Linearised plus saturation;
partial.}
\item Paper~III --- discrete-time response and inertial effects.
\emph{Speculative.}
\item Paper~IV --- predictions and tests of the gravitational
sector. \emph{Mixed.}
\item Paper~V --- the gauge sector $(T, I, \psi)$ and emergent
electromagnetism. \emph{Speculative; coefficients
uncalibrated.}
\item Further exploratory notes (Papers~VI and onward) address
coupling, cosmology, quantum phenomenology, particle structure,
and condensed-matter analogies. \emph{These are increasingly
speculative; we say so where it applies.}
\end{enumerate}

The reader should expect the support for our claims to weaken
as the series progresses. The present paper, Paper~I, claims
