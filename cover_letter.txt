==========================================================================
SUMMARY
==========================================================================

I introduce the Discrete Drainage Dynamics (DDD) framework: a strictly local, conservative, sign-respecting update rule on a random geometric graph (Poisson-disk lattice) that produces a non-negative reserve field R_i. Numerical experiments show that, in the static spherical sector, R(r) converges to a 1/r profile to sub-percent accuracy, providing the foundation for the gravitational sector developed in Paper II. Two-source experiments confirm the expected superposition. The rule is the entry point of the ten-paper DDD series.

==========================================================================
WHY THIS MATTERS
==========================================================================

Lattice approaches to gravity (Regge calculus, causal dynamical triangulations, causal sets) typically derive geometry from combinatorics. DDD takes the orthogonal route: a local conservation law on a fixed graph that produces a Newtonian profile by construction, with no metric input. The result is a minimal substrate from which the rest of the series builds gravity, electromagnetism, and cosmology.

==========================================================================
NOVELTY
==========================================================================

First-principles introduction of the conservative drainage rule R_i = (1/d_i) sum_j R_j with non-negativity floor. Analytical derivation of the static spherical solution. Numerical demonstration of 1/r convergence on Poisson-disk lattices with N up to 16,000. Two-source superposition test with equipotential overlay.
